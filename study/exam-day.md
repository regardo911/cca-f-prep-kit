## The Night Before, the Morning Of, the Exam

**Night before.** Open your Chapter 3 pattern-recognition cheatsheet. Read it once. Sleep eight hours. Do not study past 9 p.m. The exam is recognition under pressure; cramming hurts more than it helps.

**Morning of.** Light review of your weakest domain only, twenty minutes maximum, no new material. Eat a full breakfast: the exam is one hundred twenty minutes with no scheduled break, and a hungry brain misses the close calls. Test your webcam and the proctoring environment thirty minutes before the scheduled start, with the proctor's setup checklist open in another tab. Have your government ID ready for proctor verification. Water bottle within reach. Phone in the next room.

**During the exam.** Read each stem twice before looking at the answers. Identify the pattern (Chapter 3) before evaluating options. Eliminate the obvious distractor first. Pick the architectural answer over the knob-turn answer. Do not second-guess once you commit; momentum matters across sixty questions and one hundred twenty minutes. Flag any question where you genuinely cannot pick; come back at the end if time allows.

**After.** Results are reportedly delivered within two business days, with a domain-by-domain breakdown. The breakdown tells you which domain you scored highest and lowest in, which is useful even if you pass on the first attempt. If you fail, the cooldown opens for a retake; pay the ninety-nine dollars, retake. Anthropic has not published a retake wait period — check the current retake policy in the exam portal when your result lands. The Brazilian architect's six-month wait was his choice, not a stated policy.

## What to Do If You Fail by Four Questions

If your first attempt comes in four questions short of passing, do not panic. The Brazilian architect is in this position. The right response is structured.

First, read the domain-by-domain breakdown Anthropic delivers with the result. The breakdown tells you which domain held you back. If the bottom domain is Domain 5, you are in good company; close-fail candidates land here disproportionately.

Second, redo the BUILD STEP for the bottom domain. Not the chapter; the BUILD STEP. Recreate the artifact from scratch in a fresh directory. The recreation reps are what convert recognition into reflex.

Third, retake this mock chapter in two weeks. Score by domain again. If your bottom-domain score has lifted by ten points or more, schedule the retake. Anthropic has not published a retake cooldown; the six-month wait the Brazilian architect described was his choice. Check the exam portal for whatever wait window applies the day your result lands.

Fourth, treat the second attempt as the real attempt. The first sit was a paid practice run; the recognition reflex you walked in with was not yet calibrated. The second sit is calibrated. Most candidates who fail the first attempt by four questions pass the second attempt by margins.

The cost of two ninety-nine-dollar attempts is one hundred ninety-eight dollars. The cost of the architectural skill the chapters built into your reps is unbounded; you carry it through the rest of your career as an architect. The exam is the smaller cost.
