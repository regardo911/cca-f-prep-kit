# Mock Scoresheet and the Go/No-Go Rule

Score the sixty-question mock by hand here, or run
`python3 score_mock.py my-answers.txt` from the repo root and let it do the
arithmetic. Both land on the same rule.

```
Domain 1 (16 Q):  ____ / 16  =  ____%
Domain 2 (11 Q):  ____ / 11  =  ____%
Domain 3 (12 Q):  ____ / 12  =  ____%
Domain 4 (12 Q):  ____ / 12  =  ____%
Domain 5 ( 9 Q):  ____ /  9  =  ____%
TOTAL    (60 Q):  ____ / 60  =  ____%
```

The decision rule:

**80% or higher overall:** ready to schedule the live exam this week. Your weakest domain on the mock is your one focused review area. Re-read its chapter once and book the date.

**70 to 79% overall:** one more week of focused prep. Identify your bottom two domains; re-read those chapters; redo the BUILD STEP for each; retake this mock at the end of the week. If the second pass clears 80%, schedule the live exam.

**Below 70% overall:** redo Chapters 4 through 9 in their full BUILD STEPS, ship every project to your prep repo, and retake this mock in two weeks. Better a calibrated two-week delay than a failed first attempt and a six-month wait.

The four-question margin from Chapter 1 lives in this scoring page. If you are at seventy-eight percent overall, you are roughly four questions from a passing scaled score on the live exam. Drill the bottom domain. Get it to four questions on the right side of the line.

Compare the diagnostic-domain you wrote on a sticky note in Chapter 1 to your weakest domain on this mock. If they match, the chapter for that domain has not landed yet — re-read it in full. If they differ, the domain you originally feared was overcalled and a different domain is now the priority. The deliverable from Chapter 1 closes the loop here.
