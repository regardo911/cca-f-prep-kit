# Chapter 11: Cert Positioning

The money chapter, and the only folder here with no code in it. Chapter 11
contains zero fenced blocks. Its deliverables are two paragraphs and a
spreadsheet, and they are deliverables all the same.

## What you build

A LinkedIn About paragraph that names the architectural patterns the cert
grades and points at a repo somebody can open, plus five personalized outreach
DMs.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [linkedin-template.md](linkedin-template.md) | the About paragraph, verbatim |
| [outreach-dm-template.md](outreach-dm-template.md) | the DM, verbatim |

## The one command

None. This one is typing, and no script will do it for you.

The nearest thing to a command is opening the two templates, filling in
`[Name]`, `[role]` and your own handle, and sending five messages.

## Editing the LinkedIn paragraph

Three things make it work, and all three are worth keeping when you edit it. It
names the architectural patterns the cert actually grades. It names a verifiable
repo. And it ends with "I can defend each project in an interview," which is an
open invitation to a technical screen.

Cut the eight projects down to the ones you actually built. A list of eight you
cannot walk through beats a list of three you can, right up until the moment
somebody asks about the fourth one.

`github.com/[your-handle]/cca-f-prep` is yours to fill in: your prep repo, not
this one. That is the book's convention, and any name works.

Add the credential itself in the Licenses and Certifications section too, not
just in About. Issuing organization Anthropic, the issue date, and the
credential ID when it lands. If you have not sat it yet, list it as in
preparation with your exam date.

## Sending the DMs

Fill in `[Name]` and `[role]` for real. Do not blast-template. Five
well-targeted messages beat fifty generic ones, and the difference shows in the
first line.

Search LinkedIn for "Solutions Architect", "AI Systems Architect", "Senior
Software Engineer (AI)" or "Sales Engineer (AI)", filtered to companies you
would actually join. The cert is the conversation hook, the repo is the proof,
and the sixty-second loom is what you send when they say yes.

## Where the cert lands, and where it does not

**Lands:** Solutions Architect. AI Systems Architect. Senior engineer with an AI
specialization. Sales engineer at an AI vendor, in the advisory kind of SE role.
Production engineer pivoting toward architecture. Internal mobility at a
partner-network company.

**Does not:** pure ML research. Frontend. Data analysis. Junior engineering.
Product management. Demo-and-quote sales engineering.

Read the job description before you send. If it names architectural patterns,
the cert helps. If it is about demos and pricing decks, it is noise.

## What success looks like

At least one substantive reply inside seven days, or the pattern that means the
same thing: recruiter outreach that mentions the cert, profile views from
architect-track titles, a technical screen on the calendar.

Zero replies in seven days is information too, and it is about your targeting or
your first line. It is not about the cert.

## How to run it on your own career

Track the five in a spreadsheet with six columns: date sent, recipient, role,
company, reply, outcome. Read it after two weeks. Five DMs is a small sample, so
treat one reply as a signal to send five more of the same kind, and zero as a
signal to change the targeting before you send anything else. There is no CSV in
this folder for that, because six column headings do not need to be a file.

Tag the corresponding commit in your own `cca-f-prep`
`ch11-credential-deployed`.

## The numbers, and the one that does not exist

$99 per attempt. Not $100; study guides quoting a round number are quoting
something nobody published. Two attempts is $198, and that is the worst case for
someone paying out of pocket. Most readers pay $0 through a partner-network
employer, or $99 once.

Anthropic has not published a recertification policy. There is no stated
validity period, and the six-month renewal cycle circulating on Reddit is
speculation being repeated as policy. Budget as though a renewal exists, revise
when Anthropic says something, and walk away from any guide that prices this out
as a forever-cost.

There is no salary figure here, and that is deliberate. The cert is a few weeks
old, there is no survey, and any specific number you read about its salary
impact is somebody guessing. Adjacent architect certs have shown lifts in the
five-to-fifteen-percent range in the surveys that do exist. That is directional
and it is all anyone honestly has.

The defensible version of the argument does not need a number. The cert is a
forcing function for eight projects you can defend, and the projects are the
thing that converts. The badge gets you the conversation.
